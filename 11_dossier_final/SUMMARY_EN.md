# The C204 "time machine" attributed to John Titor — summary in English

This is an English summary of the programme's synthesis. The full reports are in French; the
[simulator](https://wil-pe.github.io/c204-dossier-scientifique/simulateur.html#lang=en) and the
[site](https://wil-pe.github.io/c204-dossier-scientifique/en/) have English versions.
Full synthesis (French): [DOSSIER_SCIENTIFIQUE_C204.md](DOSSIER_SCIENTIFIQUE_C204.md).

Nothing claimed in the original documents is taken as true. They are exhibits to be analysed.

---

## What this is

In November 2000 an anonymous poster calling himself "TimeTravel_0", later "John Titor", wrote on US forums that he
came from 2036. He circulated photos and "operating manual" pages of his machine: the **C204 Gravity Distortion
Time Displacement Unit**, said to weigh about 500 lb (227 kg), to sit in a car, and to be driven by two
"microsingularities".

This repository examines those documents end to end: an archive of the surviving copies, a reconstruction of the
object as described, a physics and engineering review, nine simulations, a parametric 3D model, twelve safe
experiment designs, a review of research from 2001 to 2026, two adversarial reviews, and a design for the closest
machine that known physics would let you build.

## The verdict

1. **The documents describe an object, not a machine.** They show, consistently, the outside of an elongated case
   with a hinged lid, two circular wells, a control panel and a wired remote. Nothing documents the inside: the
   35-callout cutaway does not match the case, has no legend, and its origin is unknown.
2. **The machine as described is ruled out by known physics**, by 10 to 30 orders of magnitude. Five teams
   recomputed the figures independently. None of the errors we later found in our own work affects this.
3. **The story's numbers were not picked at random.** The stated power (300–500 MW), "the size of an electron" and
   "the mass of a mountain" agree with each other: they describe a primordial black hole of a few 10¹² kg, as
   popular science presented it in the 1970s–1990s. Whoever wrote the posts had read real physics. But those numbers
   cannot fit in a 227 kg unit, which the author himself conceded in March 2001: with that mass, "the unit could
   not be moved".
4. **The periphery can be built; the core cannot.** Atomic clocks, voting computers, a gravimeter, cooling, a remote
   and an emergency stop all exist, though at full size they do not fit in the case. Micro black holes, their
   confinement, "mass-altering" electron injection, an X-ray vent and a "gravity lock" have no possible realisation.
5. **No known mechanism moves an object into the past.** Travel into the future is real and measured, but tiny at
   human scale: the best machine buildable in the same case gains about half a nanosecond per hour, nearly 18
   orders of magnitude short of the claimed "ten years per hour".
6. **What remains open is documentary and theoretical, not technical**: the provenance of two documents, the true
   scale of the case and the identity of the author; and, in physics, the status of chronology protection and the
   fate of the interior of rotating black holes.

## The numbers

| What the story says | What the calculation says |
|---|---|
| A unit of about 500 lb (227 kg) | The stated 300–500 MW requires two black holes of **3.9–5.1 × 10¹² kg each** |
| X-rays vented out of the back | Gamma rays of 12–16 MeV in all directions: about 3,400 Gy/s at one metre, a **lethal dose in about a millisecond** |
| 100–120 °F around the unit | That temperature corresponds to 0.15–0.30 kW dissipated, a million times less than stated |
| A pull of 1.5 to 2 g | Two such black holes would give **53 to 69 g** at one metre |
| A field reaching 10 to 12 feet either way | The whole Kerr structure of such a black hole fits in about 10⁻¹⁵ m. A one-metre region with closed timelike curves needs 1.35 × 10²⁷ kg, about 0.7 Jupiter masses |
| Ten years per hour | A dilation factor of **87,660**. LEP's electrons at CERN reached about 89,000, for particles of 10⁻³⁰ kg. For 227 kg it would take 1.8 × 10²⁴ J |
| Batteries supply the electricity | Enough for the electronics (1.4–5.5 kWh per trip). Making the singularities would take 2.6 × 10²⁸ to 9 × 10²⁹ J |
| A breakthrough at CERN "in about a year" (2000) | First LHC beam in 2008; no micro black hole has ever been produced |

Two claims are incompatible with each other, independently of any hardware: a felt acceleration of at most 2 g
over a few metres bounds the time-dilation factor to within 10⁻¹⁵ of 1, twenty orders of magnitude away from
87,660.

## The ten questions

| # | Question | Answer | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What was the architecture of the C204 according to the documents? | An elongated case with two circular wells, a control panel and a wired remote; six systems named in the text. The interior and the scale are not documented | High for the exterior; low for the interior and the scale |
| 2 | What role do the two singularities play? | In the story: the source of gravity, "main" and "steering". In physics: no causal role is possible; their effects are confined to about 10⁻¹⁵ m | High |
| 3 | How much energy is needed? | A few kWh for what can be built; 2.6 × 10²⁸ to 9 × 10²⁹ J to make the singularities | High, as an order of magnitude |
| 4 | Can electromagnetic fields produce the gravitational effects described? | No: 2.6 × 10²⁸ J of field energy would be needed for 2 g at one metre, or 3.5 × 10¹⁸ T for strong curvature | High |
| 5 | Do known solutions of Einstein's equations match some aspects? | Yes, for isolated aspects (Kerr–Newman, van Stockum–Tipler, double Kerr, Gott, Morris–Thorne). None combines a compact source, modest mass and accessible closed timelike curves | High |
| 6 | Can a local distortion of spacetime be produced at macroscopic scale? | Yes in the weak sense: clocks measure it down to a millimetre of height. Not in the strong sense with human means | High |
| 7 | Is there a consistent way to move an object in time without violating causality? | Into the future: demonstrated. Into the past: consistent formulations exist (self-consistency, Deutsch's model, post-selection) but no buildable mechanism; physics has not settled it | High for the future; medium for the past |
| 8 | Which components could be built today? | Clocks, voting computers, remote, emergency stop, electron gun, coils up to 20 T, vacuum vessel, cooling up to about 100 kW, gravimeter, battery. At full size they weigh 209 kg and need 127 L, against 35 L of usable volume: only a reduced 36 kg version fits | High for buildability; low for the budget |
| 9 | Which components are fundamentally impossible? | The singularities within 227 kg and their manufacture, their confinement, "mass-altering" injection, steering the spin axis, the X-ray vent, the gravity lock, any safe state | High |
| 10 | Can an experimental version be built? | No scaled-down version of the effect: there is no effect to scale. But a blind test bench for any candidate device, and a 36 kg instrument in the same case | High in principle; medium for performance, which is unmeasured |

## What can actually be built

Three constructions are described in the build guide (in French:
[GUIDE_CONSTRUCTION.md](../10_machine_optimisee/GUIDE_CONSTRUCTION.md)). Costs and durations are estimates
without quotes; we have built none of them.

| Build | What it does | Estimated cost | Estimated time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A · Inert replica** | Nothing: a display model faithful to the documents | €140–240 | 15–25 h |
| **B · C204-R1 Lite** | Compares time references, runs three voting computers, counts cosmic muons | €350–730 | 40–80 h |
| **C · Full C204-R1** | The same with four chip-scale atomic clocks and a fibre-optic gyroscope | ≈ €49k | 300–600 h |

The real "time displacement" is about 0.5 ns per hour at 130 km/h heading east. It is undergone by any object on
the same trip: a sandbag of the same mass shows the same value. The instrument computes it; it does not cause it,
and its own miniature clocks are too noisy to measure it.

## How it was made, and what that is worth

- Eight specialised AI agent teams, driven with Claude Code, plus a red team with one job: prove the machine cannot
  work, then attack the institute's own results.
- All scripts re-run; outputs were reproduced to the byte during validation.
- The second red-team pass logged **58 corrections** to our own work, four of them blocking. Examples: Hawking
  emission underestimated by a factor of 21 in the first baseline; a mass budget given without a volume budget;
  a simulation in which 17 of 28 "tests" validated nothing. Each report ends with its correction log.
- 446 references were verified online from metadata and abstracts. In an audit of 89 citations, 71 were sound,
  8 approximate, 2 misused and 8 could not be retrieved; none was invented.

## Limits

- No experiment was run and nothing was built. The experiment designs and the R1 machine exist on paper only.
- The simulations are illustrative. Each isolates one piece of physics; none models a device.
- All source documents are low-resolution copies of copies with an uncontrolled chain of custody.
- Every absolute dimension of the case rests on assumed reference objects (length 0.85 m, plausible range
  0.65–1.25 m). The physics conclusions do not depend on it; the volume budget does.
- References were checked from abstracts, not full texts.
- We do not know who the author was, where the cutaway drawing comes from, or how the "bent laser" photo was made.
- Enthusiasts analysed these images as early as 2003. What is new here is the end-to-end, source-based and
  reproducible treatment, not the idea of examining them.
